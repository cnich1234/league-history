# Week 2 Preview: One Week of Evidence and Already Some of You Look Cooked

One week in and the league has sorted itself into two groups: five people who think they have proven something, and five people who are quietly hoping nobody checks the tape. Both groups are wrong in different directions. Kevin dropped 191.66 on the field and looks like the best team in the league. Chris R scored 102.96 and looks like a man who let a stranger set his lineup. Everyone else landed somewhere in the mushy middle where fantasy football lives most of the time.

## I Cashed @ WSOP x8 (Ernie) vs Devin (Devin)

Ernie is 1-0 after putting 172.16 on Chris R, which sounds impressive until you notice Chris R scored 102.96 and a decent bowling team could have covered that. Caleb Williams went for 41.3 and Derrick Henry added 37.3, so Ernie's day was real. It was also a day where he left **Patrick Mahomes and 25.7 points on his bench**, started Kyle Pitts for a clean 0.0, and ran a Chargers defense that also managed 0.0. Winning by 69 while actively sabotaging three roster spots is the most Ernie thing that has ever happened.

Devin took his own 135.40 past Mike B and made two free-agent pickups this week, swapping in Evan McPherson and Aaron Jones, which is the behavior of a man who watched his own roster and did not care for it. He is 13-8 against Ernie in the regular season, 14-10 once you count the playoffs, and owns the biggest blowout in the series, a 217.4 to 121.8 evisceration back in 2018 that he has presumably described to someone at a party.

The model gives Ernie 134.0 to Devin's 127.9 and a 56% edge, which is close enough to a coin flip that either of them could win it by leaving the wrong man on the bench. **Take Ernie**, narrowly, and take him knowing he will probably sit somebody who scores thirty.

## Etienne Ass (Chris R) vs JSN Derulo (Kevin)

This is the game where the schedule stops being funny. Chris R opened with 102.96, the lowest score in the league by nearly seventeen points, and now draws the team that hung 191.66 on somebody. His career record is 6-9. His entire fantasy football existence is fifteen games long and nine of them are losses.

Kevin is the problem. Christian Watson went 33.7, Justin Jefferson 31.2, and the whole thing added up to the highest score of week 1 by a comfortable margin. He has two championships and a career record 44 games over .500, which is the best number anyone in this preview is going to post.

The one thing in Chris R's favour is that Kevin has not actually beaten anybody awake yet. He drew Brandon in week 1, hung 191.66 on him, and now gets the only team in the league that failed to reach 110. Two soft weeks in a row is not a resume, it is a schedule.

Projection says 142.5 to 120.3, a 71% shot and the widest gap on the board. **Kevin wins.** Chris R's only path is Kevin forgetting which day it is.

## Gronkey Punch (Mike B) vs MiOK (Mike R)

Two 0-1 teams that lost in the two most annoying ways available. Mike B lost by 5.38 to Devin. Mike R lost by **0.32 points** to Chad, which is not a loss so much as a small psychological injury he will carry into November.

Mike B's week featured Jaylen Waddle putting up 1.2 points as a starter, which is the kind of number that makes you check whether the guy actually played. He is 92-109 for his career, finished 10th last season at 4-11, and is 8-12 against Mike R in the regular season, 9-13 all in, including a 2023 game where he scored 47.5 and lost by 147.6. That is both the biggest blowout this matchup has ever produced and the lowest score of Mike B's fourteen seasons in this league, which is quite the daily double.

Mike R has three rings and a career record 20 games over .500, but he also started the Rams defense for 1.0 and benched Jayden Daniels for 21.7 in a game he lost by a third of a point. Think about that for a second. Any one of a dozen small decisions flips that result.

Model likes Mike B 134.5 to 127.3, 57%. **Slight lean Mike B**, mostly because Mike R has to be feeling it after that.

## Bud Light from Target (Brandon) vs Seal Team Nix (Austin)

The reigning champion is 0-1 and it was not close. Austin scored 119.54 against Chris N and got beaten by 36. His best player was D'Andre Swift at 34.4, his defense scored 0.0, and five rings did not appear to help at any point.

Brandon lost too, but he lost to the 191.66 buzzsaw, which is the acceptable way to go 0-1. His 147.40 would have beaten six of the other nine teams in the league that week. That is genuinely unlucky and he is allowed to say so once before it becomes tiresome.

The regular-season series is dead level at 14-14 across 28 meetings, the longest genuinely even rivalry in this league. Austin leads 16-14 all-time only because he has beaten Brandon both times it mattered, in the playoffs, which Brandon is welcome to bring up never. Last year's meeting was a 191.9 to 184.6 shootout that Brandon took. The model has Brandon 141.3 to 127.6 and a 64% edge, the second-widest gap on the board.

**Take Brandon.** Austin at 0-2 would be the funniest outcome available to us this week, and the numbers agree.

## Token Effort (Chris N) vs Deebo My Lemons (Chad)

Chris N is 1-0 after 155.42, with Jahmyr Gibbs at 35.6, Jalen Hurts at 30.7 and Chris Olave at 29.2. He also started the Texans defense, which scored **negative five points**, and still won by 36. When you can post a minus-five on a starting slot and win comfortably, things are going well.

Chad won 139.16 to 138.84 over Mike R, the ninth-closest finish in eighteen years of this league. Josh Allen went 41.7 for him. He also benched Jaxson Dart for 32.6 and Isaiah Likely for 27.8, started Colston Loveland for 0.0 and Harrison Mevis for 1.0, and won anyway by a third of a point. Chad's roster management this week was a coin flip that happened to land right.

History is not kind here. Chris N is 16-8 against Chad in the regular season and 20-10 across all thirty meetings, playoffs included. He also owns the biggest blowout in the series, 169 to 93 back in 2014. The model splits it closer than the record suggests, 141.7 to 136.9, a 55% edge.

**Chris N, narrowly.** Chad dropped a Jacksonville defense that had just scored him 13 and picked up San Francisco instead, which is either sharp planning or exactly the sort of decision that costs a man a game by a third of a point.

## Lock of the Week

Kevin beats Chris R and it is not close.

The bet with actual value is the bench. Six twenty-point players sat out week 1 on somebody's bench: Chad left Jaxson Dart (32.6) and Isaiah Likely (27.8), Ernie left Patrick Mahomes (25.7) and Kyle Monangai (22.4), Mike R left Jayden Daniels (21.7), and yes, Chris N left Brock Purdy at 27.1, so this is not a sermon from higher ground.

Three of those six sat in the same game, the one Chad won by 0.32. Two of them were Chad's own, and he won anyway, which is either luck or a horseshoe lodged somewhere permanent. Nobody in this league loses to a better team, they lose to the guy they left on their own bench, and eighteen years in, there is no sign anyone has learned it.

## The Market

Three people are trading and forty-seven shares are out there, which is a small market with a big opinion: more than half of it sits in one receiver who caught two passes in week 1. Somebody is either very early or very wrong, and Sunday decides which.

What anybody is holding is their own business, same as a bet before it locks. If the portfolios were public we would all end up owning the same four players and calling it strategy.
