# Week 3 Preview: Three Unbeatens, Three Winless, and Brandon Doing Everything Right

Two weeks of evidence in and the league has already produced its defining image: Chad winning back-to-back games by 0.32 and 0.36 points. Not one lucky escape. Two. Consecutively. The man is 2-0 with a combined winning margin of 0.68 points, which is less than a single Mike Evans reception, and he is going to spend the rest of the season being described as a 2-0 team.

Elsewhere: Kevin is at 362.74 points for the season, 57.86 more than anyone else. Brandon is 0-2 with the third-most points in the league. Ernie scored 99.62 and left Patrick Mahomes on his bench for the second consecutive week of doing something like this. Let's go around the room.

## JSN Derulo (Kevin) vs I Cashed @ WSOP x8 (Ernie)

Kevin is 2-0 and it has not been competitive either week. He put 171.08 on Chris R, the high score of week 2, and got there because **Jaxon Smith-Njigba scored 43.5** — which, for a manager whose team is named JSN Derulo, is the closest thing to divine confirmation this league offers. Jonathan Taylor added 29.2, Joe Burrow 20.18, and Justin Jefferson managed 8.5 while nobody noticed or cared. Kevin's 362.74 points for is the best mark in the league by a comfortable distance, and his 258.7 points against is only fourth-lowest. He is not being carried by a schedule. He is just better than you.

Ernie, meanwhile, scored **99.62 — the only team in the league to fail to reach triple digits in week 2** — and did it while Patrick Mahomes sat on his bench for 36.98. That is the same figure Matthew Stafford put up for Devin in the game Ernie lost by 46.16. Ernie started Caleb Williams for 7.72 instead. He also started DJ Moore for **negative 0.1 points**, benched Josh Downs (14.2) and Xavier Worthy (13.5), and got exactly 1.0 from the Buccaneers defense. Swapping Mahomes in for Caleb Williams was worth 29.26 points and Ernie declined it. Ernie has now left Mahomes on his bench in both weeks of the season and won and lost one each, so the lesson has not been forced on him yet.

The head-to-head is where it gets uncomfortable. Ernie is 12-9 down against Kevin in the regular season across 21 meetings — and **13-10 all-time once you count the playoffs**, because they have met twice in the playoffs and split them 1-1. The most recent meeting was last year's week 16 playoff game, which Kevin took 114.5 to 97.9. Ernie's career mark is 70-89, nineteen games under .500, and he still made the playoffs as the 2-seed last season at 10-5 before finishing 4th, which remains the most Ernie sequence available.

Model says Kevin 152.5 to Ernie 133.3, a 68.6% edge and the widest gap on the board at -272. **Kevin, and it is not interesting.**

## MiOK (Mike R) vs Debo66 (Devin)

The best game on the board, and the numbers barely separate them. Mike R is 1-1 after posting 166.04 on Mike B, the second-highest score of week 2, built on **Davante Adams at 40.5 and Amon-Ra St. Brown at 36.2** — 76.7 points from two receivers. Dalton Kincaid added 22.5 and the Rams defense went 11.0, a considerable improvement on the 1.0 it gave him in week 1.

And he still managed to bench a 39.78, because **Jared Goff outscored Mike R's starting quarterback Jayden Daniels 39.78 to 16.74** and watched the whole thing from a folding chair. A week after losing by 0.32 with Daniels benched for 21.7, Mike R started Daniels and benched the guy who scored forty. There is a version of this man who is 2-0 and it requires no new players, just a different mouse click. Brock Bowers and A.J. Brown also combined for 0.0 on his bench, so the roster is deep enough to be embarrassing in multiple directions.

Devin is 2-0 with 281.18 points and beat Ernie 145.78 to 99.62 behind **Stafford's 36.98, Travis Kelce's 26.1 and Kenneth Walker's 25.8**. He did it while starting the Chiefs defense for **negative two points** and Alec Pierce for 2.1, which means Devin scored 145.78 with two starting slots actively working against him. He also benched Bhayshul Tuten for 14.2 and Puka Nacua for 0.0, which is the correct order of those two decisions.

This is the longest series in the preview: **31 regular-season meetings, Mike R up 16-15 by a single game.** Stretch it to all 33 including playoffs and Mike R leads 18-15, because he is **2-0 against Devin in the postseason** — including last year's week 17 game, 130.1 to 101.4. Devin has never beaten this man when it counted. He also owns the wrong end of the series' biggest blowout, a 173.2 to 90.2 mauling in 2024 that Mike R presumably still describes in full sentences.

Model has Devin 135.5 to Mike R 129.3 and gives the home side just 43.8%. **Take Devin at -155**, and understand you are betting against a man who is 2-0 in the only games in this rivalry anyone remembers.

## Etienne Ass (Chris R) vs Seal Team Nix (Austin)

Chris R has the two worst numbers in the league: **214.26 points for, the lowest, and 343.24 points against, the highest.** He is 0-2 after going 6-9 in his first regular season, so the losses are piling up faster than the seasons. In week 2 he scored 111.30 against Kevin's 171.08 and the standout was Jake Ferguson at 20.3, which is a nice bounce from the 2.6 Ferguson gave him in week 1 and still nowhere near enough. Lamar Jackson went 16.8. Josh Jacobs — acquired from Ernie in that week 1 trade — scored 0.0 from the bench, which is at least consistent with the 0.0 he posted in week 1. Mark Andrews sat for 10.9 while the Ravens defense started and scored 2.0.

Austin is the reigning five-time champion and he is 1-1, having just beaten Brandon by **6.06** in the second-closest game of week 2. Ja'Marr Chase finally showed up for 26.5 after a 3.2-point opener, DeVonta Smith went 28.7, James Cook 22.9. He also left **Jonah Coleman (14.8), TreVeyon Henderson (13.6) and Adonai Mitchell (12.3) on his bench** — 40.7 points from three players — while starting David Montgomery for 4.4 and Michael Wilson for 3.8 in a game he won by six. Five rings and he is still winning games by the width of a bench decision.

The history here is short and one-sided: **Chris R is 0-2 against Austin, all of it last season, zero playoff meetings.** Week 8 of 2025 was a 203.2 to 127.0 demolition, the biggest blowout of the pair's brief acquaintance. Week 13 was 117.3 to 111.3, a six-point loss, which is the closest Chris R has come to beating him. Chris R has never beaten Austin and has also never beaten Ernie, so he is compiling a list.

Model: Austin 133.7 to Chris R 127.5, 43.8% for the home side. **Austin at -155**, though a Chris R upset here is the single most likely route to something funny happening this week.

## Token Effort (Chris) vs Gronkey Punch (Mike B)

Chris lost by **0.36 points**. Not a typo, and not the first time that specific flavour of misery has visited this league — Chad beat Mike R by 0.32 the week before and has now done it to two different people on consecutive Sundays. Chris put up 133.66 with Jahmyr Gibbs at 23.3, Chris Olave at 22.6 and **Brandon Aubrey kicking 22.0**, which is the most productive thing a kicker has done in this league all year. Jalen Hurts added 20.16. Malik Nabers gave him 1.1 and Tucker Kraft 3.5, and Harold Fannin sat on the bench for 10.4, so a tight end swap wins that game outright with 6.54 points to spare.

Mike B is 0-2 and the stat line is grim in a familiar way: 250.44 points for, 301.44 against, and a 120.42 in week 2 that was third-lowest in the league. **Christian McCaffrey 22.6, Jaylen Waddle 22.8** — an enormous improvement on the 1.2 Waddle posted in week 1 — and Trey McBride 18.1. Then Drake Maye for 8.02 while Justin Herbert sat for 9.88, Eddy Pineiro for 5.0, the Eagles defense for 3.0, and Rachaad White benched for 14.3. Mike B's career record is 92-109 and he finished last season 4-11 in tenth place, so 0-2 is not a deviation from the pattern, it is the pattern arriving on schedule.

The series is genuinely close: **Mike B leads the regular season 14-13**, but Chris is **2-0 in the playoffs**, which flips it to **15-14 Chris all-time.** That is the tidiest example in this whole preview of why you should always ask which record someone is quoting. Mike B leads when it does not matter and trails when it does. The most recent meeting was week 5 of last season, a 126.7 to 121.6 Chris win, so add a narrow one to the pile.

Model splits it as close as it gets: Chris 141.6, Mike B 140.9, a 50.7% edge and the only pick'em on the board. **Lean Chris**, mostly because Mike B has to score 140 to get there and he has not scored 140 yet this season.

## Deebo my Lemons (Chad) vs Bud Light from Target (Brandon)

Chad is 2-0 by a combined 0.68 points and his week 2 was an absurd document. **Josh Allen scored 46.82**, the highest single-player total in the league this week and the second straight week Allen has led the league for him. The rest of the starting lineup produced 87.2 between nine players. Saquon Barkley scored 3.0. Harrison Mevis kicked 4.0. Javonte Williams went 8.0. Chad's team is Josh Allen and a rumour, and he is undefeated. His points for sits at 273.18 and his points against at 272.5 — a **season-long differential of 0.68, exactly equal to his combined margin of victory**, which is the neatest arithmetic this league has produced in eighteen years.

Brandon is the story of the season so far and the story is theft. He is **0-2 with 291.86 points, the third-most in the league**, ahead of both 2-0 teams below Kevin. He has conceded 342.18, second-worst behind only Chris R. In week 2 he scored 144.46 — a total that would have beaten five of the other nine teams that week — and lost by 6.06. **Dak Prescott went 37.76, CeeDee Lamb 36.3, the Vikings defense 17.0**, and it bought him nothing. Cameron Dicker kicked 2.0 and Dallas Goedert caught enough for 1.4, and his only bench miss, Courtland Sutton's 5.5 behind Jameson Williams' 5.3, cost him 0.2. The best lineup-setter in the league lost anyway. Brandon has now lost to the week 1 high score and then lost a six-point game. He is allowed to be loud about it.

The history is a genuine rivalry: **Brandon up 13-12 in the regular season across 25 meetings, and dead level 15-15 across all 30 once you add the playoffs**, where they have met five times and Chad holds a 3-2 edge. The most recent was last year's week 17 playoff game, which Brandon took 133.6 to 131.1 — a 2.5-point win, because of course it was. The series' biggest blowout is Chad's, an 202.3 to 116.2 dismantling in 2018.

Model: Chad 138.8 to Brandon 136.4, 52.4% home. **Slight lean Chad**, but if Brandon loses another one-score game he should be allowed to appeal to a governing body.

## The Side Games

Week 2 was the first week the Book, the daily and the Trophy Room all counted, so here is the table going into week 3.

**The Book.** Mike R leads the season off a single slip: 1-0, $100 wagered, up $1,434. Devin is 2-4 and up $1,011, Brandon 2-2 and up $684.50. Austin is **2-9** and still somehow in the black at $84.61. The only losing ledger in the league belongs to Chris, down $105.08 at 3-5, which is also the most wins anyone has. He is winning bets and losing money, which takes a special kind of stake sizing.

**The Daily.** Chris is the defending champion at 151.12, with Brandon 9.86 back at 141.26 and Kevin third at 130.78. Kenneth Walker was in five of the seven lineups. Mike R, Mike B and Ernie did not enter, so three managers have yet to find out how badly they would do.

**The Trophy Room.** Kevin took 26 points of hardware in one week, ten more than anybody else. Ernie took 8, every one of them from Low Score, Worst QB and Worst WR. Those two play each other this week, which means the Trophy Room is about to have a very predictable Tuesday.

## Lock of the Week

**Kevin beats Ernie.** The 68.6% line is the widest on the board, Kevin is the only team in the league averaging over 180 a week, and Ernie just posted the only sub-100 score of week 2.

The real lock is the bench. **Two of the six biggest individual scores in week 2 were on somebody's bench**: Jared Goff's 39.78 on Mike R's and Patrick Mahomes' 36.98 on Ernie's. Goff outscored nine of the league's ten starting quarterbacks from a folding chair, Dak Prescott's 37.76 included, with only Josh Allen's 46.82 clearing him. Mike R won by 45.62 anyway. Ernie lost by 46.16. If Ernie had started Mahomes over Caleb Williams he would have gained 29.26 and still lost by 16.9, which is the bleakest sentence in this preview: **he made the worst decision of the week and it did not even cost him the game.**

## The Market

Five people are now trading and 98 shares are out there. The concentration has barely moved, though: **78 of those 98 shares sit in just two players — Ja'Marr Chase at 43 and Colston Loveland at 35.** Chase has now answered his 3.2-point opener with 26.5, so whoever got in early looks clever. Loveland has scored 1.3 and did not start, which looks like a different kind of conviction.

Everything past that is a rounding error: Saquon Barkley at 10 shares off a 3.0-point week, Matthew Stafford at 5 after scoring 36.98, and Bo Nix at 2. The market has correctly identified that Stafford is good and has put five shares behind the insight.

Who holds what stays private, same as a bet before it locks. And worth saying plainly, because somebody always gets this wrong: holding shares in a player is not the same as rostering him. **Ja'Marr Chase plays for Austin. Colston Loveland is on Chad's bench. Saquon Barkley starts for Chad, Stafford for Devin, Nix for Austin.** Whatever the market thinks of those five men, their fantasy points went to the managers who actually own them.
